Principles

Calm screens, loud numbers.

People come to Billback annoyed at their power bill. The interface stays quiet, white and orderly, so the one number that matters (what they get back) is the loudest thing on the screen.

1. Lead with the money

Every screen opens on a dollar figure, with a range when it's an estimate. Green means money back. Red means money leaving that doesn't have to.

2. Show what we did

Indigo marks work we've done: prefilled, ready, one tap left. Amber marks the steps only the customer can take: a call, a signature, a tick.

3. The last click is theirs

We never switch, sign or submit for anyone. Buttons say exactly what opens ("Open CEA form, filled in") and the trust line says where the numbers came from.

4. Date every figure

Rates change several times a year. Each number shows its source and date in a meta line beneath it.

Logo

The B that pays you back

The lower bowl of the B is a return arrow. Use the mark alone at 32px and below. Keep clear space equal to the arrow's height on every side. Don't recolor it, outline it, or set it on photos.

Favicon, app and touch icons are in brand/png/

Voice

Plain, on your side, a little bit of edge

Do

  • "You paid $104 more for CEA in August than SDG&E's own power."
  • "We filled it in. You press Submit."
  • "Get your power bill back."
  • Say ranges when it's an estimate: "$20–25 a month".
  • Name the button after what opens: "Open CEA form, filled in".

Don't

  • "Stick it to SDG&E!" Angry copy reads as a scam. The numbers carry the edge.
  • "Save up to 50%!" No unbounded claims, no figure without a date.
  • "We'll switch you." We never switch, sign or submit for anyone.
  • Exclamation marks, emoji in UI, stock photos of light bulbs.
  • Fake social proof. Toasts only show real, consented results.
